Part I — Foundations

1. About Sri Krishna University
Sri Krishna University is an imaginary private, self-financing university in Tamil Nadu, India, created for this handbook. Its policies are modelled on the general practices of well-known Indian universities and colleges, and on the regulations of bodies such as the University Grants Commission (UGC), the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) and the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC). Wherever a figure such as a fee, a percentage or a deadline appears, it is an illustrative value that a real institution would confirm and adjust before adopting.
Vision. The University aims to be a trusted centre of learning where students from every background grow into capable, ethical and socially responsible professionals.
Mission. Sri Krishna University commits to delivering high-quality teaching, encouraging research and innovation, building strong links with industry and the community, and giving every learner a safe and fair environment in which to succeed.
Core values. The University works by the values of integrity, respect, inclusiveness, excellence, accountability and service. Every policy in this handbook should be read in the light of these values, and where a rule is silent or unclear, the authorities of the University will decide in the way that best serves them.
Scope of this handbook. The policies apply to all students, faculty, non-teaching staff, contract workers, visitors and partners while they are on campus, using University facilities, taking part in University-organised activities off campus, or using University digital systems. Joining the University, whether as a student or an employee, means accepting these policies.

2. Governance and Administration
The University is governed by a Board of Management led by the Chancellor, with the Vice-Chancellor as the chief academic and executive officer. The Registrar looks after administration, records and legal compliance, and the Controller of Examinations is responsible for the conduct of all examinations and the publication of results. The Dean of each School leads academic planning for the departments in it, and each department is headed by a Head of Department.
Academic decisions on syllabi, regulations, new programmes and the academic calendar are taken by the Academic Council, which includes senior professors, Deans, external experts from academia and industry, and student and alumni representatives where appropriate. The Board of Studies of each department prepares and revises the curriculum at least once every three years so that it stays current with industry and research.
Day-to-day oversight of student welfare is shared among the Dean of Student Affairs, the Chief Warden, the Proctor and the Director of Counselling. Statutory committees, namely the Anti-Ragging Committee, the Internal Complaints Committee, the Equal Opportunity Cell, the Grievance Redressal Committee and the Internal Quality Assurance Cell, work independently and report directly to the Vice-Chancellor.

Part II — Academic Policies

3. Admission Policy
Eligibility. Admission to undergraduate programmes requires a pass in the higher secondary examination (Class 12) or its recognised equivalent, with the minimum subject combination and marks set by each programme. For example, B.Tech programmes require Mathematics and Physics with at least 50 percent aggregate, and B.Com or BBA programmes accept any stream. Postgraduate programmes require a recognised bachelor's degree with at least 50 percent marks, and Ph.D. admission follows the UGC regulations on entrance test and interview. Lateral entry into the second year is open to diploma holders in the relevant discipline.
Selection. Selection is made on merit, using the qualifying examination marks, a recognised national or state entrance test where one is required, and, for some programmes, a personal interview or aptitude test. The University publishes the admission schedule, the number of seats and the selection criteria on its website before applications open. Seats are allotted without discrimination on grounds of religion, caste, gender, region or disability.
Reservation and special categories. The University follows the reservation rules of the Government of Tamil Nadu and the Government of India for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Backward and Most Backward Classes, and persons with disabilities, as applicable to the type of seat. Separate seats are kept for sports achievers, children of defence personnel, and a limited number of management and NRI quota places, each filled through a transparent and recorded process.
Documents and verification. Applicants must produce original mark sheets, the transfer and conduct certificates, the community certificate where relevant, the migration certificate for students of other boards, an identity proof and recent photographs. Admission remains provisional until the documents are verified, and any false or forged document leads to cancellation of admission at any stage, with no refund of fees paid.
Withdrawal and refund. Fee refunds on withdrawal follow the UGC refund guidelines. A student who withdraws at least 15 days before the commencement of the programme receives the full fee less a small processing charge. Refunds then reduce in stages, and no tuition fee is returned after 30 days from the start of classes. Original certificates are returned within a week of the student clearing all dues.

4. Academic Programmes and Curriculum
The University offers undergraduate, postgraduate, diploma and doctoral programmes through its Schools of Engineering and Technology, Computer Applications and Information Science, Management and Commerce, Arts and Sciences, and Health and Allied Sciences. Programmes follow the Choice Based Credit System, which lets students choose electives, minors and value-added courses alongside their core subjects, in keeping with the National Education Policy 2020.
The academic year is divided into two semesters of at least 90 working days each, with an optional summer term for remedial courses and short-term certifications. The academic calendar, giving dates for reopening, internal tests, examinations and holidays, is released before every academic year. Teaching uses a mix of lectures, laboratories, tutorials, projects and seminars, and the medium of instruction is English unless a programme states otherwise.
Every undergraduate programme includes an internship or industrial training of at least four weeks, a final-year project, and courses on environmental studies, professional ethics and constitutional values. Students may earn credits through approved online courses such as SWAYAM and NPTEL, up to the limit set by the Academic Council. Students may also seek an academic credit transfer from other recognised institutions through the Academic Bank of Credits.

5. Attendance Policy
Regular attendance is essential to learning. A student must attend at least 75 percent of the classes held in every course, counting theory, laboratory and tutorial sessions separately, in order to be allowed to write the end-semester examination. Attendance is recorded by the faculty member in every period and displayed to students every month so that no one is surprised at the end of the term.
A student whose attendance falls between 65 and 74 percent may apply for condonation with a valid reason, such as illness supported by a medical certificate, a family emergency, or representing the University or the State in an official sport, cultural or academic event. The Head of Department recommends the request and the Dean decides on it, and a condonation fee is charged for each course. Students with less than 65 percent attendance are not eligible for condonation and must repeat the course when it is next offered.
Absence on approved On-Duty grounds, such as inter-college events, University-sanctioned workshops and placement drives, is counted as present when the permission is taken in advance and the participation certificate is submitted within a week of the event. Parents or guardians of students with low attendance are informed in writing.

6. Examination and Evaluation Policy
Continuous internal assessment. Each theory course carries 40 percent of its marks for internal assessment, made up of two or three periodic tests, assignments, quizzes, seminars and attendance, and 60 percent for the end-semester examination. Laboratory courses are assessed on regular records, performance and a viva. Projects are evaluated through reviews with a guide and an external examiner.
End-semester examinations. The Controller of Examinations conducts the examinations in line with the published timetable and issues hall tickets to eligible students only. A student must secure at least 40 percent in the end-semester examination and at least 50 percent overall in the course to pass it. Questions are set by external paper setters, scripts are coded, and evaluation is done by at least one internal and one external examiner under the supervision of a chief examiner.
Revaluation and re-totalling. Students may apply for photocopies of their answer scripts, for re-totalling and for revaluation within ten days of the declaration of results, on payment of the prescribed fee. If the revaluation result differs by more than a set margin, a third examiner reviews the script and the better of the awarded marks is considered final.
Supplementary and arrear examinations. Students who fail a course may appear for it in the next scheduled examination, and a special supplementary examination is held for final-year students so they can finish without losing a year. Absence due to a medical emergency or a serious family event may be treated as a fresh attempt on proper proof, subject to the approval of the Controller.
Malpractice. Any attempt at malpractice, including copying, carrying unauthorised material or mobile phones into the hall, impersonation, or tampering with scripts, is dealt with by the Examination Malpractice Committee. Punishments range from cancellation of the examination in question to cancellation of all papers of the semester, debarment for one or more semesters and, in grave cases, dismissal from the University. The student is always given a fair chance to explain before a decision is taken.

7. Grading, Promotion and Graduation
The University uses a ten-point absolute grading system. Each course is awarded a letter grade and corresponding grade points, from which the Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) and the Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) are calculated, weighted by course credits.
| Grade | Marks (%) | Grade points | Meaning |
| O | 90–100 | 10 | Outstanding |
| A+ | 80–89 | 9 | Excellent |
| A | 70–79 | 8 | Very good |
| B+ | 60–69 | 7 | Good |
| B | 50–59 | 6 | Above average |
| C | 40–49 | 5 | Average |
| F | Below 40 | 0 | Fail, course to be repeated |
| AB | — | 0 | Absent |
A student is promoted to the next year when they have cleared at least two-thirds of the credits of the earlier year and have completed the required attendance. A programme is considered complete when the student has earned all required credits, cleared all arrears, completed the internship and project, and held a CGPA of at least 5.0. Degrees are classified as First Class with Distinction for a CGPA of 8.5 and above, First Class for 6.5 and above, and Second Class for the remainder, subject to completing the programme in the stipulated time with no more than one attempt in any course.
Provisional certificates, consolidated mark sheets, transcripts and degree certificates are issued by the Controller of Examinations. Degrees are conferred at the annual convocation, and students unable to attend in person may receive their degree in absentia.

8. Academic Integrity and Plagiarism Policy
Academic honesty is the base of a university's reputation. Students and staff must submit only their own work, give credit to every source they use, and never fabricate data, results or citations. Sharing assignments for copying, getting someone else to write an assignment, or using AI tools to produce work without the instructor's permission and disclosure are all treated as breaches of integrity.
In line with the UGC Regulations on Promotion of Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism (2018), all theses, dissertations and research papers are checked with approved similarity-detection software before submission. Similarity of up to 10 percent is acceptable, similarity between 10 and 40 percent requires a revised submission, and similarity above 40 percent leads to disciplinary action, which may include a failure grade or withdrawal of the work. The Departmental Academic Integrity Committee examines every case, and the student has the right to be heard and to appeal to the Vice-Chancellor.

9. Library Policy
The Central Library serves all students and staff with printed books, journals, e-resources, digital databases and study spaces. It is open from 8.30 a.m. to 8.00 p.m. on working days, and the reading hall stays open until late at night during examination weeks. Members must present their identity card to enter and to borrow, and silence must be maintained in reading areas.
Undergraduate students may borrow up to four books for 14 days, postgraduate students and research scholars up to six for 21 days, and faculty up to ten for a semester. Books can be renewed once if no one else has requested them. A late fee of a few rupees per day is charged on overdue books, and lost or damaged books must be replaced or paid for at current cost. Reference books, rare collections and the latest journal issues are for reading in the library only.
Access to e-journals, e-books and national digital libraries such as N-LIST and the National Digital Library of India is provided on campus and through remote login. Users must follow the licence terms of every publisher and must not download in bulk, share passwords or redistribute content.

10. Research and Intellectual Property Policy
The University encourages faculty, scholars and students to pursue research that addresses real problems. Seed grants, travel support for conferences, access to advanced laboratories and fee waivers for publications in reputed journals are provided through the Research and Development Cell. Research must follow ethical norms, including approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee for studies involving human participants, animals or sensitive data, and from the Biosafety Committee where relevant.
Authorship in publications is given only to those who have made a real intellectual contribution, and guides and supervisors must not be added or removed for reasons of rank or favour. Publishing in predatory journals or conferences is discouraged and is not counted for promotion, scholarships or evaluation.
Inventions, software, designs and other intellectual property created by staff or students using University resources are owned jointly by the creator and the University, unless a sponsor agreement says otherwise. The Intellectual Property Cell assists with patent filing, copyright, trademarks and technology transfer, and revenue earned from commercialisation is shared, with the larger share going to the inventors. Students' original coursework and personal projects done without University resources remain the student's own.

11. Internal Quality Assurance Policy
The Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) works to plan, monitor and improve the quality of teaching, learning, research and administration. It prepares the Annual Quality Assurance Report, coordinates preparation for NAAC and NBA accreditation, and promotes the use of data in decision-making.
Feedback is collected every semester from students on courses and teachers, and yearly from alumni, parents, employers and staff. The results are reviewed by Heads of Department and the Deans, and an action-taken report is shared back with the participants. Academic and administrative audits are carried out once every two years by internal and external experts, and teachers are expected to take part in at least one faculty development programme each year.

Part III — Student Life and Welfare

12. Student Code of Conduct and Discipline
Students are expected to behave with courtesy, honesty and respect, on campus and off it, and to remember that they represent the University. They must carry their identity card at all times, follow the dress code announced by the University, which is decent and appropriate for a learning environment, and keep to the timings and rules of classrooms, laboratories, hostels and libraries.
Acts that are treated as misconduct include disrupting classes, damaging property, theft, gambling, possession of weapons, bullying, abusive or threatening behaviour, misuse of University names or logos, taking part in political agitation on campus without permission, and breaking the law. The Proctor investigates complaints, and the Disciplinary Committee decides the outcome after giving the student a chance to respond in writing and in person.
Penalties depend on how serious the act is and may include a warning, a written apology, community service, a fine or recovery of damages, suspension of hostel or transport facilities, suspension from classes, withholding of results or certificates, and, for the gravest offences, expulsion. Students have the right to appeal to the Vice-Chancellor within 15 days of the decision. Criminal offences are reported to the police.

13. Anti-Ragging Policy
Sri Krishna University has zero tolerance for ragging in every form, following the UGC Regulations on Curbing the Menace of Ragging in Higher Educational Institutions (2009) and the directions of the Supreme Court. Ragging means any conduct, verbal, written or physical, that teases, humiliates, harasses or causes fear or distress to a student, including forcing a student to do something against their will.
Every student and parent must sign an anti-ragging affidavit at the time of admission. Fresh students are protected by an Anti-Ragging Committee and Squad, who patrol the hostels, canteens, bus stops and other vulnerable areas, and by an orientation programme that explains the law. A 24-hour helpline and an online complaint portal are available, and the national anti-ragging helpline is displayed prominently on campus.
Anyone found guilty is liable to punishments that may include suspension from classes, withholding of scholarships, cancellation of admission, rustication for up to four semesters, expulsion and a police case under the relevant sections of the law. Those who witness ragging and fail to report it, and those who try to shield the guilty, are also dealt with. Complaints can be made confidentially, and no one who reports ragging in good faith will suffer retaliation.

14. Prevention of Sexual Harassment Policy
The University is committed to a campus free from sexual harassment, in accordance with the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013, and the UGC (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal of Sexual Harassment of Women Employees and Students in Higher Educational Institutions) Regulations, 2015. The policy protects students, staff and visitors of all genders.
An Internal Complaints Committee (ICC), headed by a senior woman faculty member and including faculty, staff, student representatives and an external member from an NGO or legal background, receives and investigates complaints. A complaint can be made in writing within three months of the incident, which may be extended on good cause, and the identity of the people involved is kept confidential. The inquiry must be completed within 90 days, and interim relief such as a change of class, hostel or reporting line is available when needed.
Where the complaint is proved, action may range from a written apology and warning to suspension, termination of employment or expulsion, and the committee may recommend compensation to the complainant. Retaliation against a complainant or a witness is treated as a separate offence, and false complaints made with malicious intent are also punishable. The University holds regular awareness sessions for every new batch of students and for all employees each year.

15. Equal Opportunity and Anti-Discrimination Policy
The University does not allow discrimination on grounds of caste, religion, gender, sexual orientation, language, region, disability, economic background or any other personal characteristic, in admissions, teaching, evaluation, employment, housing or access to facilities. An Equal Opportunity Cell, working along with the SC/ST Cell, OBC Cell and Minority Cell, monitors compliance with the UGC (Promotion of Equity in Higher Educational Institutions) Regulations, supports students from disadvantaged groups with remedial classes, mentoring and scholarship guidance, and handles complaints of discrimination.
A student who feels discriminated against can approach the cell in person, by email or through the online portal. The cell inquires into the matter promptly, and the authorities act on its recommendations. Offensive remarks, jokes or conduct that demean a person's identity are treated as misconduct and dealt with under the code of conduct.

16. Inclusion and Disability Support Policy
In line with the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, the University works to make learning and campus life accessible to everyone. Persons with benchmark disabilities are given the reserved share of seats, fee concessions where the law provides, and reasonable accommodations such as extra time in examinations, a scribe or reader, large-print or accessible digital materials, and the use of assistive technology.
The campus is being made barrier-free through ramps, lifts, accessible washrooms, tactile paths, signage and reserved parking, and classrooms and hostels for students with mobility needs are allotted on the ground floor. A Disability Support Cell coordinates these services and trains staff and student volunteers. Students are encouraged to register with the cell at the start of the year, and information about a disability is shared only with those who need it to provide support.

17. Grievance Redressal Policy
Every student and staff member has a right to raise a complaint and have it heard fairly. The University follows the UGC (Redress of Grievances of Students) Regulations, 2023. A complaint may concern academic matters, examinations, fees, hostel or transport, facilities, staff behaviour or any other matter that affects a student's rights.
A grievance is first raised with the concerned teacher, mentor or Head of Department, who must try to resolve it within seven days. If it is not resolved, the student can approach the Grievance Redressal Committee, which meets at least once a month, gives a written decision within 30 days, and keeps a record of all grievances. A student who is not satisfied can appeal to the Vice-Chancellor, and then to the University Ombudsperson, an independent person appointed in line with UGC norms. Complaint boxes and an online portal allow anonymous complaints on non-personal matters.

18. Hostel Policy
Separate hostels for men and women are provided on campus with furnished rooms, a dining hall, Wi-Fi, a gym, laundry, a common room and round-the-clock security. Rooms are allotted on merit and distance of residence, with priority for first-year students, outstation students and students with disabilities. A refundable caution deposit and annual hostel and mess fees are payable at the time of allotment.
Hostel timings are announced by the Chief Warden, with students expected to be inside by the closing time at night and to sign out and in for outings. Girls' hostels follow the same safety-based timings as boys' hostels in line with University rules on equality, and late returns require prior written permission. Visitors may meet students only in the visitors' room during visiting hours, and parents or authorised guardians may stay in the guest house.
Cooking appliances, alcohol, tobacco, drugs, weapons and ragging are not allowed in the hostels. Students are responsible for the cleanliness and safe use of their rooms and for any damage they cause. The Chief Warden may inspect rooms with notice, in the presence of a warden or student representative. Mess committees with student members review menus and hygiene every month, and the food is checked in line with FSSAI standards.

19. Transport Policy
The University runs buses on routes covering the main parts of the city and nearby towns, subject to enough demand. Students and staff who use the service must pay the annual transport fee, carry a transport pass, board and alight only at designated stops and follow the driver's and attendant's instructions. Buses are fitted with GPS tracking, CCTV and first-aid kits, and drivers are trained and checked for fitness regularly.
Students who bring their own vehicles must hold a valid driving licence, wear helmets or seat belts as required by the Motor Vehicles Act, and park only in designated areas. Speed on campus is restricted, and reckless riding or driving leads to disciplinary action and loss of parking permission. Underage driving is not permitted.

20. Health, Counselling and Wellbeing Policy
The campus has a health centre with a qualified doctor, nurses and an ambulance, tied up with a nearby multi-speciality hospital for emergencies. Basic consultation and first aid are free of cost, and every student is covered by a group medical insurance scheme paid for through the annual fees. Students with long-term health conditions are encouraged to inform the health centre so that proper support can be given in confidence.
The Counselling Centre offers free and confidential support for stress, anxiety, relationship concerns, academic pressure and personal difficulties, led by trained counsellors and psychologists. Every student is also assigned a faculty mentor who checks in regularly. Teachers and wardens are trained to notice signs of distress and refer a student for help. In line with the UGC guidelines on student wellbeing and suicide prevention, the University holds awareness programmes each semester, displays national helpline numbers around campus, and treats any report of risk as an urgent matter.
Students who are ill for a long time may apply for medical leave with proper certificates, and the University will work with them to recover missed academic work wherever possible. An outbreak of infectious disease may lead to temporary rules on masks, isolation and online classes, as directed by public health authorities.

21. Drug-Free, Alcohol-Free and Tobacco-Free Campus Policy
The manufacture, possession, sale, use or sharing of narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances is forbidden on campus and in University-run activities, in line with the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985. Alcohol and tobacco products are likewise not allowed, and the campus is declared a smoke-free zone under the Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act (COTPA), 2003, with no sale of tobacco within 100 yards of the campus.
The Anti-Drug Committee organises awareness drives, supports students who ask for help to quit, and works with the police and de-addiction centres. A student who seeks help voluntarily is treated with care and not punished for that alone. Anyone found selling or supplying drugs, or found guilty repeatedly, faces expulsion and a report to the police.

22. Student Clubs, Sports and Extracurricular Policy
Co-curricular and extracurricular activities are valued as an important part of education. Students may join technical, literary, cultural, entrepreneurial and social service clubs, the National Service Scheme (NSS), the National Cadet Corps (NCC) and the Youth Red Cross. Each club has a faculty advisor and a student council, receives a yearly budget, and must submit an activity report at the end of the year.
The University has grounds and indoor facilities for cricket, football, volleyball, basketball, athletics, badminton, table tennis and chess, along with a gym and a yoga hall. Students who represent the University, State or country in sports and cultural events are eligible for attendance relief, special coaching, scholarships and academic support. Student council elections, where they are held, are conducted in a fair manner under rules framed by the Dean of Student Affairs and in line with the Lyngdoh Committee recommendations. Events, fests and competitions require prior permission, and fund raising from outside needs approval of the Dean.

23. Student Leave Policy
A student who needs to be away from classes must apply in advance, through the mentor and the Head of Department, except in an emergency, when the student or the parent should inform the department as soon as possible. Casual absence of up to three days at a time may be approved by the class advisor, and longer leave needs the Head's approval. Medical leave requires a certificate from a registered medical practitioner and must be submitted within seven days of return.
A student who needs a break for health, personal or family reasons may apply for a semester or year break, up to a maximum of two semesters across the entire programme, with approval from the Dean. The student rejoins the programme in the next appropriate semester, and any required fee adjustments are made. Leave does not itself count towards attendance, which is calculated separately under the attendance policy.

24. Fees, Scholarships and Financial Aid Policy
Tuition and other fees for each programme are announced before admission and are payable by the dates set by the University, in instalments where allowed. Fees include tuition, examination, library, laboratory, development, student welfare and insurance charges, and hostel, mess and transport are charged separately. Late payment attracts a fine, and students with unpaid dues may be barred from examinations and from receiving certificates. Fee structures are fixed in line with the fee committee of the State government and are not raised during the course by more than a modest percentage each year, and any change is announced at least three months before it takes effect.
The University supports deserving students through merit scholarships for top rankers, sports and cultural scholarships, need-based fee concessions, scholarships for first-generation graduates and for students from economically weaker sections, and a freeship for students who lose a parent or guardian during their studies. Students are guided to apply for government schemes, such as the National Scholarship Portal and the Post-Matric scholarships for SC/ST and minorities, and for education loans from banks. Scholarships continue each year if the student maintains the required CGPA and attendance and has no disciplinary record. A scholarship obtained through false information is withdrawn and recovered.

25. Placement and Career Development Policy
The Training and Placement Cell prepares students for work through aptitude, communication and technical training, mock interviews, résumé clinics, industry talks and internships, and invites employers for campus recruitment. All final-year students who are eligible, which means no pending arrears and the minimum CGPA the company sets, may register for placement.
A student who accepts an offer is generally not eligible for further offers, except under the dream-company rule, which allows a student to attend drives of companies offering a package substantially higher than the earlier one. Students must conduct themselves professionally and must not misbehave, make false claims in their résumés or withdraw from a selection process without good reason, since that damages the reputation of the whole batch. The University supports students who prefer higher studies, entrepreneurship or competitive examinations through an incubation centre, a career guidance unit and a coaching programme.

Part IV — Campus and Operations

26. Campus Safety and Security Policy
The University maintains round-the-clock security with trained guards, CCTV coverage in all public spaces, controlled entry and exit, visitor registration and well-lit pathways. Women's safety is given particular attention through patrols, panic buttons, a helpline and coordination with the local police station. Students and staff must carry their identity cards and report suspicious people or incidents immediately to the security office.
All buildings meet fire-safety norms, with extinguishers, alarms, marked exits and regular fire drills at least twice a year. Laboratories follow safety rules on chemicals, electrical equipment and waste, and users must wear the right protective gear. A Disaster Management Plan covers fire, flood, earthquake and other emergencies, and trained volunteers and first-aid teams are available. Any accident or injury must be reported to the department and the health centre without delay.

27. IT Acceptable Use and Cybersecurity Policy
The University's computers, network, Wi-Fi, learning management system, email and software licences are provided for academic and official purposes. Users must keep their passwords private, use strong passwords and not share accounts, must not install pirated software, and must not try to bypass the University's firewall and filters, scan the network or gain access to systems they are not authorised to use.
Activities that are strictly forbidden include hacking, spreading malware, accessing or sharing obscene, hateful or illegal content, cyberbullying, spreading false information, online gambling, and breaking copyright through unauthorised downloading. In line with the Information Technology Act, 2000, the University may monitor network traffic for security reasons, and may suspend accounts that violate the policy. Personal devices connected to the network must have up-to-date security software. Serious offences are handled under the code of conduct and may be reported to the cyber crime authorities. Students pursuing cyber security or forensics must use only authorised laboratory environments for practical work and never test tools on live systems without written permission.

28. Data Privacy and Records Policy
The University collects personal information about students, staff and alumni only for lawful, stated purposes such as admission, academics, welfare, employment and legal compliance. In keeping with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, information is stored securely, accessed only by authorised staff, shared with outsiders only when required by law or with consent, and kept no longer than needed.
Students and staff have the right to see and correct their own records and to withdraw consent for non-essential uses. Academic records, such as mark sheets and transcripts, are kept permanently. Photographs and videos taken at events may be used for official publicity, and a person who objects may contact the Registrar. Any data breach must be reported at once to the IT department and the Registrar, who will inform affected people and the authorities as required. Requests for information from the public are handled in line with the Right to Information Act, 2005, where it applies.

29. Social Media and Communication Policy
Students and staff are free to use social media but must remember that what they post can reflect on the University. They should not post anything abusive, defamatory, discriminatory or confidential, should not claim to speak for the University without approval, and should respect the privacy of classmates and colleagues, avoiding recording or posting images of people without their consent. Official University accounts are managed by the Corporate Communications Office, and any statement to the media must be cleared by the Registrar or the Vice-Chancellor.
Teachers and students should keep their relationships professional online, use official channels such as the University email and learning platform for academic communication, and avoid private messaging that could be misread.

30. Environmental Sustainability Policy
The University is committed to a green and low-carbon campus. It promotes solar power and energy-efficient lighting, rainwater harvesting, sewage treatment and reuse of water, waste segregation at source, composting of food waste, safe handling of e-waste and chemical waste, plastic reduction and the planting and care of trees. The campus encourages cycling, walking, electric vehicles and shared transport, and holds an annual environment audit and energy audit.
Every student takes a course on environmental studies, and clubs and the NSS carry out cleanliness drives, tree-planting and awareness programmes. All users are expected to switch off lights and equipment when not in use, avoid wasting water and paper, and keep the campus clean.

31. Visitors, Events and Facilities Use Policy
Visitors, including parents, guests and vendors, must register at the gate, state the purpose of their visit and wear a visitor pass, and may enter academic areas and hostels only with permission. Photography on campus requires approval, and commercial filming, solicitation and distribution of pamphlets are not allowed without written permission.
University halls, grounds and classrooms may be booked for events through the Registrar's Office, with a faculty or staff member responsible for each event. Events must end at the stated time, must not disturb classes or hostel residents, and must follow rules on safety, crowd control, sound levels and cleanliness. External organisations hiring University facilities pay the prescribed charges and are responsible for any damage.

Part V — Faculty, Staff and Institutional Ethics

32. Faculty Recruitment, Service and Appraisal Policy
Faculty are recruited in accordance with UGC and AICTE qualification norms, which require a master's degree with at least 55 percent marks and NET, SET or a Ph.D. for the post of Assistant Professor, and higher qualifications and experience for Associate Professor and Professor. Posts are advertised openly, applications are screened and shortlisted by a Selection Committee which includes subject experts from outside, and appointments are made on merit. New appointees serve a probation of one to two years.
Faculty are expected to teach their assigned workload, mentor students, take part in research and in departmental and University activities, keep their knowledge up-to-date, and set a good example of conduct. Performance is reviewed each year through a self-appraisal, student feedback and a review by the Head of Department, in line with the Academic Performance Indicator framework of the UGC, and promotions follow the Career Advancement Scheme. Faculty on duty must not take private tuition for pay from the University's own students and must not engage in outside work that causes a conflict with their duties without permission.
Salary and benefits follow the pay scales and allowances fixed by the Board, with provident fund, gratuity, insurance and other benefits as required by law. Teachers retire at the age of 65, and may be re-employed on contract as visiting professors based on need and merit.

33. Non-Teaching Staff Policy
Technical, administrative, library, laboratory, security and support staff are recruited through open advertisement and a fair selection process with written tests and interviews where appropriate. They are given clear job descriptions, training, uniforms where needed and annual performance reviews. Staff are expected to be punctual, polite to students, parents and visitors, keep records accurate and confidential, and follow the rules of conduct. Contract and outsourced workers are paid at least the minimum wage and are covered by the relevant labour laws, provident fund and insurance.

34. Staff Leave and Working Hours Policy
Regular working hours are fixed by the University and displayed publicly. Teaching staff are entitled to casual leave of 12 days, earned leave, vacation leave during the academic breaks, medical leave on proper proof, and on-duty leave for conferences, workshops, examination work and research. Maternity leave of 26 weeks and paternity leave are given as provided by the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, and the University's own rules, and leave for adoption and for childcare may also be given. Leave must normally be sought in advance, and leave without pay may be granted for study or other approved reasons. Staff who take leave without permission may face deduction of pay and disciplinary action.

35. Code of Ethics, Conflict of Interest and Whistleblower Policy
All members of the University must act honestly and openly and avoid any situation where personal interests clash with their duty to the University. Gifts and favours from students, parents, vendors or other parties that may influence a decision must not be accepted, family members must not be involved in recruitment, evaluation or purchase decisions concerning one another, and anyone with a conflict of interest must disclose it and step away from the decision. Bribery, favouritism, nepotism, fraud and misuse of University property are not tolerated.
A person who suspects wrongdoing, such as corruption, harassment, financial irregularity, unsafe conditions or academic malpractice, is encouraged to report it, in writing or by email, to the Registrar or the Vigilance Officer, or directly to the Vice-Chancellor if the concern involves a senior officer. The identity of the person is protected, no one who reports in good faith will be punished or harassed, and reports are examined fairly and promptly. Knowingly making false allegations is a breach of this policy.

36. Financial Management and Procurement Policy
The University's finances are managed with transparency and prudence through an annual budget approved by the Board, a Finance Committee, and audits by independent chartered accountants. All fees and income are deposited into University bank accounts and recorded through receipts, and cash handling is kept to a minimum. Every spend must be approved by the right authority within the delegation of powers.
Purchases above a stated value are made through open tenders or at least three quotations, evaluated by a Purchase Committee, and awarded on quality and price. Suppliers must follow ethical standards and may be removed from the list for poor delivery or malpractice. Assets are recorded in a stock register and checked every year, and unwanted equipment is disposed of by approved methods, including safe disposal of e-waste. Audited accounts are made available as required by law.

37. Alumni Relations Policy
Graduates are valued members of the University family. The Alumni Association registers every passing-out student, holds meets in India and abroad, publishes a newsletter, invites alumni for guest lectures, mentoring, internships and placement help, and gives them continued access to the library, career services and selected facilities. Alumni contributions, whether of money, time or opportunities, are gratefully received and recorded openly, and the University reports each year on how they are used. Alumni also have representation in the Academic Council and in the IQAC.

Part VI — Administration of This Handbook

38. Policy Review, Amendment and Interpretation
This handbook is reviewed every year by a committee led by the Registrar, and a major revision is done at least once every three years, taking into account changes in the law, UGC and AICTE regulations, feedback from stakeholders and lessons from incidents. Amendments are approved by the Board of Management or the Academic Council as the matter requires, are announced by circular and on the University website, and take effect from the date stated.
Where this handbook conflicts with a law or a binding government or regulatory direction, the law prevails. In any dispute about the meaning of a policy, the decision of the Vice-Chancellor is final, subject to the legal rights of the person concerned. Rules not covered here are decided by the competent authority in the spirit of the University's values. All disputes are subject to the jurisdiction of the courts in Tamil Nadu.
